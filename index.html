Preexisting Society + Causal Migration Diagnostic

Correction under test: Bellwether now begins as a preexisting society with canonical pre-1926 family, relationship, and residence histories. Relationship and parenthood remain orthogonal life-course systems. NPCs can be single, dating, cohabiting, engaged, married, divorced, or widowed; parenthood can occur across relationship states and persists after relationships end. There is no child ceiling. Additional children become progressively less likely as family size and parental age increase; partnership duration, birth spacing, finances, relationship stability, and partly inherited biological traits alter the opportunity space. Twins, triplets, and rarer higher-order multiples are possible.

Causal migration rule: no NPC enters or leaves Bellwether because the town needs population. A move must arise from that person's biography—work, education, relationships, family/caregiving, housing, retirement, social ties, or a genuine return-home path. Migrants remain persistent people with histories before and after the move.

Diagnostic: reproductive-age exposure → partnership opportunity → viable partner pool → partnership formation → partnered reproductive years → family-expansion opportunity → child decision → birth, plus migration and mortality.

Each diagnostic world currently runs 1926→2026 only. Bellwether itself predates 1926: older residents can enter play with established spouses, children, residences, and migration histories. The 0.7.1 life-course model can sustain/grow populations far enough into the future that synchronous 500-year batches became computationally expensive and made Safari appear unresponsive. We first validate the 100-year generational replacement behavior; long-horizon tests return after the model passes this gate.
